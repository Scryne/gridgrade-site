    13.42¢per kWh, residential

    Households in Tennessee paid an average of 13.42¢/kWh in August 2026, 26% below the US average of 18.16¢. That is up 2.5% from August 2025. Tennessee ranks #6 of 51 from cheapest to most expensive.

    Where Tennessee's electricity comes from

    In 2025, nuclear generated the most electricity in Tennessee, 38.6% of the total. Wind, solar and hydro together supplied 13.3% (US: 22.6%). Coal's share fell from 40.4% in 2015 to 23.7%.

    About these numbers

    Monthly prices are average revenue per kWh (bills divided by usage) reported to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. Per EIA's reporting rules they include customer charges, energy and demand charges, fuel adjustments, environmental surcharges and franchise fees; sales taxes added separately to your bill may not be included. Your rate depends on your utility, plan and usage. Estimate your bill or read the methodology.
