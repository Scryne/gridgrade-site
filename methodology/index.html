    Methodology

    Method version 1.0. Every number on GridGrade comes from public U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) data and is recomputed automatically when EIA publishes new files.

    Data sources

    • Monthly state prices: EIA-861M sales and revenue by state and sector, January 2010 through August 2026. EIA marks the most recent months as preliminary and revises them.
    • Utility figures: the annual EIA-861 survey for 2025: sales to ultimate customers, reliability (IEEE 1366 indices) and service territory by county.

    Price, bill and usage

    • Average price = revenue ÷ kWh sold, in cents per kWh. EIA revenue includes customer charges, energy and demand charges, fuel adjustments, environmental surcharges and franchise fees, so it is an all-in average, not a tariff rate.
    • Average monthly bill = residential revenue ÷ residential customers ÷ 12.
    • Average monthly use = residential kWh ÷ residential customers ÷ 12.
    • State figures in the headline use the latest month; bills and usage use the last 12 months so seasonal swings cancel.

    Which utility records are included

    Utility pages use EIA-861 Part A records with "Bundled" service: customers who buy both power and delivery from the utility. Delivery-only records (customers who buy power from a competitive supplier), energy-only retail marketers, third-party rooftop solar owners ("behind the meter") and EIA's statistical adjustment rows are excluded. When a utility reports separately for several balancing authorities in one state, the records are summed.

    Grades

    • Price score (0–100): the utility's average residential price ranked against every graded US utility; the cheapest scores 100 and the most expensive 0.
    • Reliability score (0–100): minutes without power per customer per year (SAIDI), excluding major event days as defined by IEEE 1366, ranked the same way; fewest minutes scores 100.
    • Overall score = 60% price + 40% reliability. Utilities that do not report reliability are graded on price alone and labeled "price-only".
    • Letter: A 80–100, B 60–79, C 40–59, D 20–39, F below 20.
    • Not graded: utilities with fewer than 100 residential customers and records EIA marks as imputed.

    Grades are relative: an A means a utility does better than most US utilities on these two measures, not that its prices are low in absolute terms. Prices reflect local fuel, climate and regulation that a utility may not control.

    Limits

    • Averages hide tariff structure: fixed charges, tiers and time-of-use rates mean your effective price depends on how much and when you use power.
    • Utility data lag by about a year because EIA publishes the annual survey in the fall after the data year.
    • County lists show utilities with any territory in the county; EIA does not publish boundaries within counties.

    Updates and corrections

    GridGrade checks EIA's files daily and rebuilds when they change. If you spot an error, open an issue on the site's public repository.
